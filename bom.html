Bill of Materials

Bill of Materials (BOM)

The Bill of Materials (BOM) is the foundational DNA of any manufactured product. It is a highly structured, comprehensive recipe that catalogs every raw material, component, and subassembly required to produce a single end item. In modern operations, the BOM is structured hierarchically as a "product structure tree," allowing MRP systems to accurately calculate the cascading multiplier effects of dependencies, manage lead-time offsets, and perform accurate cost roll-ups from the lowest level components up to the finished product.

Key Concepts

Product Structure Tree

A visual representation of the BOM. The End Item is at Level 0. Assemblies and subassemblies (parents) are made of parts or raw materials (children).

Low-Level Coding

Restructuring the BOM so that multiple occurrences of a component all coincide with the lowest level at which the component occurs, simplifying MRP calculations.

Multiplier Effect

The total quantity of a component required is its "quantity per parent" multiplied by the total demand for its parent assembly.

Cost Roll-up

The process of calculating the total cost of an assembly by iteratively adding up the costs of all its component parts from the bottom level up to Level 0.
