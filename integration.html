Chair Manufacturing Scenario
📚 What is the End-to-End Planning Chain? In real manufacturing, planning flows top-down: BOM defines what to build, Capacity defines how much you can produce, APP sets a 12-month workforce & inventory strategy, MPS disaggregates APP into weekly item schedules, and MRP explodes those schedules into component purchase orders. Every module hands off a key output to the next.

Stage 1: Bill of Materials (BOM)

The BOM is the foundation of the entire planning chain. It defines the product structure — every component, sub-assembly, quantity per parent, lead time, and unit cost needed to produce one finished Chair. Before any planning can begin, the BOM must be fully defined so that MRP can later explode end-item demand into component-level requirements.

Stage 2: Capacity Planning

Before committing to any production plan, we must know our factory's true output ceiling. Design Capacity is the theoretical max; Effective Capacity accounts for scheduled downtime, setups, and breaks; and Actual Output further reduces this by quality losses and unplanned stoppages. These constraints directly bound what the APP is allowed to promise.

Stage 3: Aggregate Production Planning (APP)

APP translates the capacity ceiling into a 12-month production strategy. The firm must decide how to match supply with demand — by hiring/firing workers (Chase), holding steady production and using inventory as a buffer (Level), or using a Mix of both with overtime/subcontracting options. The APP output is a monthly production quantity that will be disaggregated by MPS.

Stage 4: Master Production Schedule (MPS)

MPS disaggregates the APP monthly bucket into weekly end-item schedules. For January (APP = 500 chairs), we split across ~4.3 weeks. The MPS must track Projected On-Hand (POH) and Available-to-Promise (ATP) inventory to simultaneously satisfy firm customer orders and reserve capacity for forecasted demand — all while keeping POH above zero.

Stage 5: Material Requirements Planning (MRP)

MRP is the final execution layer. It takes MPS releases and the BOM to compute exactly when to order each sub-component. For each item, it nets Gross Requirements against available inventory (Scheduled Receipts + On-Hand) and offsets Planned Order Releases backward by the lead time. The result is a time-phased procurement and production schedule for every component in the BOM.

📚 MRP Explosion Logic: MPS releases 130 Chairs in Wk 2. BOM says each Chair needs 1 Seat Assembly (LT=2wks), 1 Back Assembly (LT=2wks), and 1 Leg Assembly (LT=1wk). So MRP schedules a Planned Order Release of 130 units for Seat & Back Assembly in Wk 0 (now), and Leg Assembly in Wk 1.
